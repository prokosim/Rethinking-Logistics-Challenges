## Project

The project questions road freight dominance. Its case is that trucks carry a minority of global freight but emit most transport emissions, wear roads heavily, need drivers and fit badly in dense cities. It proposes replacing trucks with a drone-based supply chain in three phases. First, a traditional warehouse with truck delivery, the status quo. Second, a traditional warehouse with drone delivery, for faster, cheaper deliveries and less congested roads. Third, a drone-tailored, compact, cylindrical or vertical warehouse with drone delivery, for lower land use and lower building OpEx. The final spread compares massing of an existing low, sprawling cross-dock with a new cross-dock of two towers linked by a long elevated deck. The questionnaire adds plans for public space spiralling around the tower and an interest in reusing existing infrastructure.

## How the student framed the challenge

- Truck dependency and road freight emissions
- Land take of warehouses
- Automation and delivery speed in supply chains
- Big-box typology lacking urban integration

## Trade-offs

- Increasing warehouse height reduces area footprint (land use) but increases embodied carbon and CapEx (questionnaire; student says both extremes are developed).
- A drone network needs very high upfront CapEx for new infrastructure but promises lower OpEx, especially for last-mile delivery (questionnaire Economy 1).
- Building vertically lowers land use but raises operational energy cost (questionnaire Energy 2).

## Decision

The cited evidence on truck emissions, drone energy per package and last-mile cost supports a phased switch to drone delivery. It ends in a drone-tailored vertical cross-dock (two towers with an elevated deck) that replaces the low, sprawling truck cross-dock to cut land use and building OpEx.
