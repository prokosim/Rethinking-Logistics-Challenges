## Project

The project starts from the problems data centres cause near towns: high water use for cooling, local heat islands, rising grid demand and large blank boxes that block residents' views. On a site in Kladno's old industrial area, two abandoned 1950s metal-factory halls planned for demolition are kept and converted into data centres A and B, and a new data centre C with a fully PV-covered roof and a greenhouse are added. Waste heat is sent to the greenhouse for year-round vegetables and, through the nearby power plant and existing networks, to housing (heating in winter, chilled for cooling in summer). The landscape replaces paving with ponds, trees and green corridors for people and small animals, and solar power covers site maintenance, public EV charging and storage for resale.

## How the student framed the challenge

- Heat waste from data centres
- Data centre energy demand and grid strain
- Urban integration and visual impact of data centres
- Adaptive reuse of industrial buildings

## Trade-offs

- Building underground would maximise green space and hide the volume but needs energy-intensive retaining walls, raising embodied CO2 and cost; building above ground is lower carbon but obstructs residents' views (questionnaire).
- Using the roof of data centre C for PV raises renewable energy generation but leaves no roof area for green roofs, so green and blue area must come from the ground plane.

## Decision

The energy and heat figures were used to justify adding a fully PV-covered data centre C and a heat-fed greenhouse, and to route remaining heat to housing through the existing power plant. Reusing the two factory halls instead of demolishing them was chosen to cut construction CO2, and the ground-versus-underground comparison led to staying above ground.
