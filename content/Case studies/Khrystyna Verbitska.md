## Project

The project starts from the shortage of last-mile micro-hubs in Prague (only Florenc and Smíchov identified) and the traffic, emission and kerbside problems caused by deliveries coming from warehouses outside the city. Using a city-wide map of brownfields and abandoned buildings, it selects an abandoned concrete structure in Háje and converts it into a micro-hub that sorts parcels and dispatches them by cargo bikes and electric vehicles. The building is stacked by floor: a ground-level logistics layer for loading, sorting and bike/EV servicing, a showroom and repair/community level above, and a public rooftop garden. Two spatial proposals were explored: a hybrid model where logistics and public programmes overlap, and a floor-separated model to avoid operational conflicts.

## Trade-offs

- Prioritising cargo-bike deliveries lowers CO2 emissions and urban traffic impact but reduces throughput and scalability, especially at peak demand (emissions vs. logistics capacity).
- Mixing public programmes with logistics increases community space and social integration but creates operational conflicts, congestion and noise at peak dispatch times (public accessibility vs. operational efficiency); addressed by comparing a hybrid layout with a floor-separated layout.

## Decision

Reuse an abandoned building on an inner-city brownfield instead of building new, which supports the embodied carbon saving and the green and public space gains. The floor-separated scheme (logistics at ground level, showroom/community above, public roof garden on top) is the one developed in the exploded axonometric.
