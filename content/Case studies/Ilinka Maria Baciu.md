## Project

The project looks at the Hostivice industrial zone, an 876,000 m² logistics area that grew next to existing housing over three decades and now separates the town from its landscape through sealed surfaces, truck-dominated roads and monotonous big-box volumes. One representative existing warehouse (about 115 x 110 m, precast concrete frame) is documented in plans, sections and construction details to understand the standard typology. Three urban scenarios (mobility links, a green buffer zone, and zoning that shifts logistics toward the airport) are tested. The design keeps the logistics function but adds a network of raised, tree-like green platforms over and between the warehouses, carrying public routes, green roofs, photovoltaics and rainwater retention, and reconnecting the interrupted green-blue corridor between Prague and Hostivice.

## How the student framed the challenge

- Urban integration of logistics next to housing
- Sealed surfaces and stormwater runoff on logistics sites
- Interrupted green-blue ecological corridors
- Rooftop renewable energy on large warehouses

## Trade-offs

- Adding public and green layers improves urban integration and biodiversity but lowers logistics efficiency and raises construction cost (questionnaire; also listed as unintended consequences).
- Heavy truck areas must stay hard and durable, limiting the permeable surface ratio; water there is routed to planted channels and retention instead.

## Decision

The site analysis and scenarios led to keeping the existing logistics function and adding a connected system of raised green platforms with PV, green roofs, public routes and rainwater retention, rather than replacing the warehouses. The rough PV estimate (56-88 GWh/year) was used to argue that the large roof area is an energy asset.
