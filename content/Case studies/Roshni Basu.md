## Project

The project starts from Prague's e-commerce logistics, where large fulfilment centres on the city edge feed fragmented, duplicated last-mile routes and a growing number of parcel lockers. Using India's Blinkit quick-commerce 'dark store' model as reference, it proposes a decentralised network of twelve neighbourhood microhubs, each serving a radius of about 2–3 km, sited by population density. A modular 'Urban Logistic PlugIn' unit (storage box with pickup counter, stackable and joinable, with solar roof, planter, bench and wiring) is adapted to two housing typologies: in the compact historic centre it is a small pavilion cluster linked to underground parking/delivery levels, and in panelák estates it becomes a market-and-hub cluster that activates underused open space. Urban, greenery and energy KPIs are proposed to measure social use, green space and solar contribution before and after.

## How the student framed the challenge

- Fragmented last-mile delivery in dense cities
- Urban integration of logistics micro-hubs
- Activation of underused residential open space
- Parcel locker clutter in public space

## Trade-offs

- Social and ecological activation of courtyards (social interaction, green space activation) conflicts with logistics presence, which brings noise, visual impact and perceived intrusion; mitigated by keeping units compact and partly underground (questionnaire).
- The courtyard/historic-centre concept gives deeper integration and social value but higher embodied carbon and CapEx because of underground construction, while the panelák concept is cheaper and has higher solar share (40–70% vs 20–40%) (questionnaire).
- Green space preservation (% kept green) competes with the footprint of inserted hub units and market functions.

## Decision

Population density and service-radius analysis led to a network of twelve neighbourhood microhubs instead of more parcel lockers; the housing-typology study led to two variants of one modular unit, hidden with underground delivery access in the compact centre and used as a market-and-greenery cluster in panelák estates.
