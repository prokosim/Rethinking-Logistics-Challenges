## Project

The project starts from an existing isolated warehouse near Prague Airport (Wakestone Logistics, approx. 5,500 m² in two halls plus 3,000 m² offices), where trucks and cars share routes and offices sit deep inside the hall without daylight. A first proposal in the existing context reorganises it into an L-shaped building that separates offices, control rooms and a restaurant from high-bay storage and docks, with distinct truck, car and pedestrian routes and a green buffer. The scheme is then moved to Pivoňská, Prague 14, between a residential area and rail/road infrastructure, where the warehouse is partly buried under a green roof that ramps down into a public park, community space, retention pond and a fox corridor along the rail line. Truck access and loading are placed on the far side from the housing, and three layout variants (L-shape, two separate buildings, campus) were compared with a KPI slider matrix.

## How the student framed the challenge

- Urban integration of logistics buildings
- Truck, car and pedestrian flow separation
- Worker comfort and daylight in warehouses

## Trade-offs

- More windows and open areas raise daylight comfort for workers but take wall space from shelving, reducing storage capacity (daylight comfort vs. storage capacity).
- Splitting the programme into two separate buildings improved traffic separation but used too much site area and isolated workers (traffic separation vs. space utilisation / worker cohesion).
- The campus layout gave more public space for residents but lowered storage and was inefficient for truck circulation (accessible green/public space vs. storage capacity and logistics efficiency).

## Decision

The L-shaped layout that groups offices, control rooms and café/shop in one wing and storage/docks in the other was kept because it scored best across the KPI matrix while separating truck, car and pedestrian flows. Relocating next to housing led to placing truck access and loading on the side away from residents, with a green-roofed, partly buried building, public park and fox corridor towards the neighbourhood.
