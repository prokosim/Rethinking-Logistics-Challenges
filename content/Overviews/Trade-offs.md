Design decisions are trade-offs between metrics. These are the ones the students identified.

## [[Adam Večeřa]]

- Adding green facades and roof (green roof/wall coverage, biodiversity) increases structural weight and therefore initial cost (CapEx) and may affect building operation (questionnaire).
- Higher CapEx share (50% vs 40%) is accepted to reduce OpEx by 60% over 30 years, with a 12-year payback (life-cycle cost KPI).
- Going vertical frees ~120,000 m² of ground for park and ecology but requires deep underground storage and parking (−24 m), which conflicts with the embodied-carbon strategy 'no underground / avoid deep foundations' listed under KPI 2.3.

## [[Adéla Berkovitz]]

- Splitting the mass into separate buildings is less financially efficient (cost) but gives human scale, better neighbourhood integration and passage for small animals (biodiversity / visual impact).
- A linear logistics flow (processing efficiency, less forklift travel) blocks a bio-corridor through the plot, so ecological elements move to the perimeter (questionnaire).
- Green roof coverage conflicts with hazard safety: the hazardous storage building cannot have a green roof.
- Rooftop car parking would save land but needs heavier columns for the larger loads (structural material/embodied carbon), so parking stays on the ground (consultation).

## [[Amy Naprstek]]

- Low thermal insulation of the lightweight relocatable blocks (thermal comfort, ability to store climate-sensitive goods) is traded against lower operational cost and lower embodied/operational carbon (questionnaire).
- Relocatability and low site impact (area covered, biodiversity gain) come at the cost of truck transport emissions for moving the blocks, which the student has not yet quantified (questionnaire 4.4).

## [[Aycha Chambashala]]

- Alternative 2 (dispersed blocks) gave variety but lowered internal traffic circulation efficiency and created unused 'dead zones' between masses.

## [[Ece Yasar]]

## [[Elsa Hauksdottir]]

- Maximising green roof coverage conflicts with minimising structural cost and embodied carbon (questionnaire).
- An open façade for public permeability and daylight conflicts with the thermal performance needed in compact, closed logistics mode, so the façade switches between the two modes.
- Robotic partitions reconfigure much faster (5–15 min vs 2–4 h) and need less labour, but cost more to operate and have higher environmental impact than manual partitions.
- A fully pneumatic envelope gives the most spatial flexibility, while a hybrid with only the roof inflatable trades some of that for structural stability.

## [[Hynek Hrabík]]
